# Cycle frameworks

Five lenses for reading any boom-and-bust. They answer different questions, so use them together.

| Framework | Source | Question it answers | Core stages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technological revolutions | Carlota Perez, *Technological Revolutions and Financial Capital* (2002) | How does a new technology reshape markets over ~50 years? | Irruption, Frenzy, Turning point (crash), Synergy, Maturity |
| Bubble anatomy | Kindleberger / Minsky, *Manias, Panics, and Crashes* | How does credit-fuelled speculation end? | Displacement, Boom, Euphoria, Profit-taking, Panic |
| Hype cycle | Gartner | How do expectations diverge from reality? | Trigger, Peak of inflated expectations, Trough of disillusionment, Slope of enlightenment, Plateau |
| Diffusion | Everett Rogers, *Diffusion of Innovations* | How fast do people adopt? | Innovators, Early adopters, Early majority, Late majority, Laggards |
| Reflexivity | George Soros | Why do prices feed back into fundamentals? | Self-reinforcing loop between prices and perceived value, until it breaks |

## Minsky's three financing postures
1. **Hedge**: cash flows cover debt service.
2. **Speculative**: cash flows cover interest only; refinancing needed.
3. **Ponzi**: cash flows cover neither; relies on asset-price rises.
The move from 1 to 3 across an economy is a classic late-cycle signal.

## Key caveat
These are descriptive models built with hindsight. They help organise evidence but do not give precise timing. Many manias run far longer than a rational observer expects.
