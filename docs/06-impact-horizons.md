# Multi-layer impact analysis, 1 to 20 years (Pillar 3)

> A structural framework. Magnitudes depend heavily on the starting point (valuations, leverage, inflation) and policy response. Treat as hypotheses to test against data, not predictions.

## Layer 1: Financial institutions and banking

| Horizon | What typically happens | Key metrics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | Mark-to-market losses on bond portfolios when rates rise (loss roughly = duration x yield change); deposit outflows to money funds; NIM squeezes if deposit costs rise faster than asset yields; lending standards tighten | Unrealised AFS/HTM losses, uninsured deposit share, NIM, loan growth |
| 3-5 years | Losses crystallise or roll off; weak banks consolidate or fail; capital rules tighten after crises; credit losses peak 1-2 years after the shock | CET1 ratios, charge-offs, commercial real estate exposure |
| 10 years | Regulatory regime resets (post-2008: Basel III, stress tests); business models shift toward fee income or shadow banking | Capital adequacy, share of non-bank lending |
| 20 years | Structural rate level, financial-system concentration and technology (digital deposits, fintech) reshape the sector | Banking-sector share of credit, concentration |

## Layer 2: Equities and asset-class prices

| Horizon | What typically happens | Key metrics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | Higher rates compress P/E multiples (long-duration growth hit hardest); defensives (staples, utilities, healthcare) outperform in risk-off; commodities respond to supply shocks; real-estate cap rates widen | Forward P/E, sector relative strength, cap rates |
| 3-5 years | Earnings catch up or fall; cyclicals lead recoveries; valuations mean-revert; credit cycle determines default wave | Earnings revisions, spreads, CAPE |
| 10 years | Starting valuation is the strongest known predictor of subsequent long-run returns (high CAPE, lower forward returns), with wide error bars; technology waves redistribute leadership (Perez cycle) | CAPE vs forward 10Y return, sector weights |
| 20 years | Returns driven by earnings growth, dividends and inflation; secular regimes (1982-2000 disinflation bull; 1966-82 stagflation) dominate | Real total return, inflation regime |

## Layer 3: Everyday consumer and microeconomic life

| Horizon | What typically happens | Key metrics |
|---|---|---|
| 1 year | Purchasing power falls if wages lag sticky inflation; mortgage-rate "lock-in" freezes home turnover; card and auto delinquencies rise; job loss concentrated in rate-sensitive sectors | Real wage growth, mortgage rate, delinquency rates, unemployment |
| 3-5 years | Prices stay higher (the level does not reverse), wages partly catch up; savings buffers depleted; refinancing and rent resets bite | Real income vs 2019 path, rent share of income |
| 10 years | Housing affordability and household wealth reflect the rate regime; generational wealth gaps widen or narrow with asset-price path | Price-to-income, homeownership by age |
| 20 years | Demographics, productivity and technology (AI, automation) dominate; pension and retirement outcomes follow the compounding path of real returns | Real wage trend, retirement-asset returns |

## Linking to the simulator
The **Stress test** tab applies a 12-month shock to inflation, Fed policy, oil and credit spreads and estimates first-order effects on equities, banks, REITs, mortgages, CPI, real wages and bank securities losses. It covers the **1-year** column of this framework; longer horizons require different models (valuation-based, demographic) and are on the roadmap.
